| 8 | 3:24 - 3:57 | So what does an effective RCA look like? It has six parts. One, a clear problem statement, with the loss in numbers. Hours, tonnes, dollars. Collect it before anything is cleaned up or thrown away. Three, a cause and effect tree. Start with the problem and keep asking why. | 13 | 6:12 - 6:56 | Now we build the tree, level by level. Why did the seals and bearings fail early? Physical causes first. One, the pump runs far from its best point, so the shaft is pushed sideways every turn. Two, poor alignment after rebuilds, including a soft foot. That means one foot of the pump base is not flat, so tightening the bolts bends the frame. Three, contaminated bearing oil. Four, a restricted seal flush, the clean liquid that cools and protects the seal faces. Then the human level. Operators throttle the discharge valve. Fitters align by straight edge. Hoses wash down onto the bearings. Nobody checks the flush strainer. | Tree animation, built top to bottom. Top box: 'Seals and bearings fail early'. | 14 | 6:56 - 7:26 | Now ask why again. This is the system level, and this is where the roots are. Nobody reviewed the pump duty when throughput changed. There is no precision alignment standard. Fix these, and people are no longer set up to fail. | Tree continues downward: a third band in teal labelled 'System (latent) causes' grows under the amber boxes with six boxes connected by lines: 'Duty not reviewed after throughput change', 'No precision alignment standard', 'Rebuild procedure gaps', 'No post-repair verification', 'Flush and oil not on inspection route', 'Failures coded only as bearing failed'. | 15 | 7:26 - 8:06 | Before we act, we test each cause against the evidence. Off its best point? The flow trend shows it. Poor alignment? The records are missing. A cause the evidence cannot support gets dropped. Then the actions. Trim the impeller so the pump runs near its best point. Fit a flush flow indicator and add it to the inspection route. Set an alignment standard, buy a laser tool and train the fitters. Seal the bearing housings, and add a rebuild checklist with a vibration reading before handover. Each has an owner and a date. Total cost, sixty thousand dollars. | Part 1: the tree returns compact; each root cause node gets a green tick as a small evidence icon flies into it (flow trend, empty records, etc.).
