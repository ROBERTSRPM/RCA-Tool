Read at your normal slow pace. Say the scene number, read the lines, pause 2 seconds, then the next scene. If you stumble, repeat the line.

Scene 1: The situation (48 s)
4 am on a Thursday, night shift. At the end of Line 3, it is the 2-hourly seal check. The operator takes 3 packs of chicken and rice and drops them in the leak tester. 2 of them leak along the seal. Everything packed since the last good check goes on hold. About 6,000 packs. The phone rings at the production manager's house. Then the QA manager's. A retailer has already complained about slack packs last month. And it is the 6th time this year. At the meeting, the general manager asks two questions. Why does this keep happening? And what is it costing us?

Scene 2: What we are trying to achieve (39 s)
We want four things. Find the real cause, not just the faulty pack. Put a dollar figure on the loss, so the fix can compete for budget. Fix it so it stays fixed. And prove it, months later, with evidence. I will do it in RCA Pilot, from a blank page. The numbers are illustrative, made up to show the method. 6 events a year, 8 hours of line stoppage each, 3,000 packs an hour, 45 cents margin a pack, and $33,100 of cash cost each time.

Scene 3: Sign in and start (30 s)
Go to w w w dot r c a pilot dot com. Demo mode needs no account. I will create an account with an email and password, so my work is kept safely and is there on any device. The AI buttons I use today need you signed in. Here is the sign-up form. Now, an account I prepared earlier, empty. I click New investigation.

Scene 4: Problem: draft from notes (44 s)
Step 1 is Problem. First, Type of failure: Equipment failure, or Process or quality failure. This one is a process or quality failure. Then the box called Draft problem statement from notes. A supervisor would jot rough notes like these. I click Draft problem statement from notes. The AI turns them into a clear statement, using only what I wrote. It does not guess a cause, and it lists what the notes left out, under Not in your notes, worth finding out. I edit the draft, press Use this draft, and then read it and correct anything.

Scene 5: Problem: line details and quality reference (19 s)
Under Type of failure, an optional Quality reference: the NCR or CAPA number. Put in NCR-2291, and this analysis can feed your site's QA and CAPA system. Further down, Line / process details: line, product, pack type and process step.

Scene 6: Loss (54 s)
Step 2, Loss. If you have no figures yet, choose Not known yet, or enter one rough yearly estimate. A rough number beats none. I enter 6 events a year, 8 hours each, 3,000 packs an hour, and 45 cents margin a pack. 8 hours at 3,000 is 24,000 packs not made. At 45 cents, that is $10,800 of lost margin, which is opportunity. Then the cash costs. The call-out is $1,200. Under Other costs I use the quick-add presets: Scrapped product $14,500. Product on hold $3,200. Customer claims and credits $6,800. Retailer penalties $5,000. Rework $2,400. Cash is $33,100. One event costs $43,900. 6 a year is $263,400. The line runs at capacity, so the lost output is lost for good.

Scene 7: Evidence (40 s)
Step 3, Evidence. Click Add evidence. Each item has a title, a type, a date, and a note on what it shows. A photo of a leaking pack, with the channel in the seal. The sealing temperature log. The inspection of the jaws. The settings and changeover record. The film specification, maintenance and QA records. And the interviews. Evidence before opinions. Keep the retained packs and the worn parts, and photograph them before anything is cleaned or replaced. Later I link each item to the cause it supports.

Scene 8: Cause tree: by hand (60 s)
Step 4, the Cause tree. The top box is the failure event. I click Why? and type the answer in plain words. Why were the packs leaking? The seals were weak along the flange. Why? The jaw ran 17 degrees low, with worn silicone. That is physical. Why was it not caught? Nothing in the maintenance plan checks jaw temperature or replaces the silicone. That is the system level, and I tick This is a root cause. Three more branches the same way. Wrong settings, where operators raised speed and cut dwell to keep up. A new film batch. And a seal check of only 3 packs every 2 hours, so a bad seal runs for hours before anyone knows. For each cause I tick the supporting evidence and mark it Verified or Ruled out.

Scene 9: Natural and forced deterioration, views (37 s)
One question I always ask where equipment is involved. Is this wear natural, or forced? Silicone on a sealing jaw wears out eventually. That is natural. But running it a year past its life, because nothing prompted anyone, is forced: wear we allowed. I set that under Type of wear. For the recipe settings, wear does not apply, so I choose Not applicable. The same tree also shows as 5 Whys, or a Fishbone grouped by the Cause area I tagged.

Scene 10: Cause coach and Challenge (46 s)
Now the AI help. The Cause coach suggests causes I may have missed, each as a hypothesis, with what would confirm it and what would rule it out. I click Suggest causes, read each one, and choose Add to tree or Dismiss. I add one that is new to me, product contaminating the flange. I dismiss gas mix, because the oxygen readings were in spec. Then Challenge my analysis. It questions the tree like an experienced reviewer. It says the film root cause has no evidence linked, so I link the film specification and mark it Verified. The AI suggests. I decide.

Scene 11: Actions (38 s)
Step 5, Actions. Corrective actions fix this failure. Preventive actions stop it happening again, one for each root cause, with an owner, due date and cost. For the jaw, a Maintenance task: check jaw temperature with a probe every week, and replace the silicone every 3 months. For settings, a Design or equipment change: lock them by recipe. For film, a Procedure or standard: supplier change control. For seal checks, more frequent checks with records, and a faster leak tester. Total cost, $20,200.

Scene 12: CAPA suggestions and quality check (23 s)
For each root cause there is also a Suggested actions button. It proposes corrective and preventive actions, says whether the wear looks natural or forced, and nothing is added until I click Add to actions. The Quality check lists any gaps, like a root cause without an action.

Scene 13: Verify (42 s)
Step 6, Prove it stopped. Savings only count once every action is Done, the review date is entered, and the failure has not recurred. Months later I mark the actions Done and enter the review date. Recurred? No. Expected events per year after the fix? 1, a cautious number. The loss falls to $43,900. The saving is $219,500, verified. Payback on $20,200 is about 1.1 months, and 40 hours of line time came back. Then Roll this fix out: Lines 1 and 2 each get a check, with an owner and a date.

Scene 14: Report (23 s)
Step 7, Report. Draft report writes the full report from everything I have entered. Management summary, one page, is what the general manager reads. I can tell it who the audience is. Then I edit the text and check every fact. When it is right, Print or Save as PDF.

That is the whole method. Describe the problem. Size the loss. Gather evidence. Ask why, down to the system. Fix it with owners and dates. Prove it stopped, and spread the fix. Try it at w w w dot r c a pilot dot com. The link is in the description.
