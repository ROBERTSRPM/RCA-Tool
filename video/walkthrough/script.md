# RCA Pilot full walkthrough: script

Voice only (the owner), calm and practical. Starts from a blank investigation, in the live app (https://www.rcapilot.com). All figures are illustrative; every value typed on screen is in `example-data.md`.

Spelling: Australian/British. Say "RCA Pilot". Say the web address as "w w w dot r c a pilot dot com". All numbers and dollar amounts are written as digits so they are easy to read aloud; read them naturally. Pace about 140 words a minute.

Note for the recording: the AI buttons need a signed-in account. Do not use the AI buttons on real data. AI answers vary each run, so adapt the spoken Cause coach, Challenge and Suggested actions lines to what appears on screen (the lines are written for the likely result).

## Scene 1: The situation (59 s, 132 words)

**Say:** "It's 4 o'clock in the morning on a Thursday, and the night shift on Line 3 is doing its 2-hourly seal check. The operator takes 3 packs off the end of the line and drops them in the leak tester, and 2 of them leak along the seal. That means everything we've packed since the last good check, about 6,000 packs, goes on hold. Then the phones start, the production manager's at home and then the QA manager's. A retailer already complained about slack packs last month, and this is the 6th time this year. When I ran the operation, this was the call nobody wanted, because I knew the general manager would ask two questions. Why does this keep happening, and what is it actually costing us?"

[SFX] Open on the steady hum and rhythmic thump of a tray sealer on a food line, with the clink of trays and a fan. Under the second line, a faint bubbling from the leak tester.

[MUSIC] Quiet, tense, low pad. Starts at 0:03, no drums.

[VISUAL] Plain title card ("It's 4 o'clock in the morning on a Thursday"), then slow stills or simple graphics: a night shift tray sealer under bright light, end-of-line check table, an operator at the end of the line pressing a pack under water in a leak tester with bubbles rising from the flange, a slack pack with a loose lid, red product-hold tags going on pallets, a bedside phone lighting up at night, then a meeting room. Caption: "Line 3 tray sealer, chilled ready meals. Illustrative example." Last 5 s: text "Why does this keep happening? What is it costing us?"

[SFX] Phone ringing at night on "Then the phones start", a second ring tone on "Then the QA manager's". Cut all sound on the final question, then silence for one beat.

[MUSIC] Fades out on the last question.

## Scene 2: What we are trying to achieve (42 s, 94 words)

**Say:** "So what do we want? We want to find the real cause and not just the faulty pack, understand the financial impact to the business, put in a countermeasure that holds, and prove that months later. I'll do it in RCA Pilot, from a blank page, and the numbers are illustrative, made up to show the method. We have 6 events a year, 8 hours of line stoppage each, 3,000 packs an hour, 45 cents margin a pack, and on average about $33,100 of cash cost each time it happens."

[VISUAL] Four large words appear one by one: "Find the cause", "Understand the financial impact", "Fix it", "Prove it". Then a simple card listing the illustrative inputs: 6 events a year, 8 hours each, 3,000 packs/hour, $0.45 margin per pack, $33,100 cash cost per event. Caption: "Illustrative figures".

[MUSIC] Tension resolves into a calm, light bed at the first word.

## Scene 4: Problem: draft from notes (51 s, 115 words)

**Say:** "Step 1 is the Problem, where a lot of investigations go wrong because somebody writes "seals bad" and everyone moves on. First I set the Type of failure, and a leaking pack is a Process or quality failure. Now, nobody at 4 o'clock in the morning writes a perfect problem statement, so a supervisor just jots rough notes, and that's what Draft problem statement from notes is for. The AI turns them into a clear statement using only what I wrote, it doesn't guess a cause, and it lists what's missing under Not in your notes, worth finding out, which is your first round of questions. Then I press Use this draft and correct anything."

[VISUAL] Problem step, all fields empty. Under "Type of failure" click "Process or quality failure". The card "Draft problem statement from notes" is open. Click into "Rough notes" and type the shift notes from example-data.md (readable speed, or paste). Click "Draft problem statement from notes". Cut while it works ("Writing the draft."). Show the draft box: Title, What happened?, Where in the process?, When did it happen?, What was the impact?, and the amber "Not in your notes. Worth finding out:" list. Edit one field by hand. Click "Use this draft". Scroll to the "1. Describe the problem" card now filled; point at the Undo link. Fix the Title and add Line or process, Site / area and Date of event (see example-data.md). Captions: "1. Rough notes", "2. AI draft", "3. Use this draft, then edit".

## Scene 5: Problem: line details and quality reference (21 s, 45 words)

[VISUAL] In the "1. Describe the problem" card, under "Type of failure", click the optional "Quality reference (NCR / CAPA number)" and type NCR-2291. ## Scene 6: Loss (63 s, 143 words)

**Say:** "Step 2 is the loss, and it's the one the general manager cares about. If you don't have figures, choose Not known yet or put in a rough yearly estimate, because a rough number beats none. Here we have 6 events a year, 8 hours each, 3,000 packs an hour and 45 cents margin a pack, so 8 hours at 3,000 is 24,000 packs we didn't make, which at 45 cents is $10,800 of lost margin. Then there's the cash, which is the $1,200 call-out plus everything that quietly adds up under Other costs. Scrapped product is $14,500, product on hold $3,200, customer claims and credits $6,800, retailer penalties $5,000 and rework $2,400. Claims and penalties don't hit every event, so these are averages across the year. That makes $33,100 of cash, so on average one event costs $43,900, and 6 a year comes to $263,400. And because the line runs at capacity, that output is gone for good."

[VISUAL] Click the Loss step. Show "I have figures (rough is fine)" and "Not known yet" (hover, do not click). Fill the figures in example-data.md. Under "Other costs per event (optional)" use the quick-add presets Scrapped product, Product on hold, Customer claims and credits, Retailer penalties and Rework, and enter each amount. Live calculation panel on the right: Lost production per event 24,000 packs; Cash costs $33,100; Opportunity loss $10,800; Cost per event $43,900; Annual loss $263,400; Hours lost 48. Highlight each as it is spoken. Show "Lost for good" at 100%. Captions: "Rough is fine", "Annual loss $263,400 (illustrative)", "Cash vs opportunity".

[MUSIC] Soft pulse as the dollar total builds, nothing dramatic.

## Scene 7: Evidence (35 s, 77 words)

**Say:** "Step 3 is Evidence, and in any plant meeting that means evidence before opinions, because everyone has a theory about those seals. Each piece gets a title, a type, a date and a note, and here we have a photo of a leaking pack, the sealing temperature log, the jaw inspection, the settings and changeover record, the film specification and the interviews. Keep the retained packs and worn parts, and photograph them before anything gets cleaned up."

[VISUAL] Click the Evidence step, then "Add evidence" six times, filling each from example-data.md (Title, Type, Date, Note). For the first item click "Add a photo" and upload any photo (or skip). Scroll the list so all six titles show with "Not linked to a cause yet" under each. Caption: "Evidence before opinions".

## Scene 8: Cause tree: by hand (73 s, 166 words)

**Say:** "Now we get to Step 4, the Cause tree, where the real thinking happens. The top box is the failure event, and we just keep asking Why? in plain words. Why were the packs leaking? Because the seals were weak along the flange. Why? Because the jaw ran 17 degrees low, with worn silicone, and that's the physical cause. If we stopped there we'd be back in a few weeks, so we ask why it wasn't caught, and nothing in the maintenance plan checks jaw temperature or replaces that silicone. That's the system level and the real root, so I mark it with This is a root cause. The other branches work the same way: wrong settings because operators raised speed and cut dwell to keep up, a new film batch, and a seal check of only 3 packs every 2 hours. For each cause I tick the evidence and mark it Verified or Ruled out, because if I can't prove it, it stays a theory."

[VISUAL] Cause tree step, view "Tree". Click the top box and type the failure event from example-data.md. Click "Why?" and type each cause as listed, building branch A fully, then B, C and D more quickly (cut or speed up in the edit). Use the "Type of cause" buttons: Physical, Human, System / Latent. Also tap one "Cause area" button (Equipment, Materials, Method, Measurement, People, Environment) on each cause, as listed in example-data.md; it shows on the card and groups the Fishbone. On the four system boxes tick "This is a root cause". Under "Evidence that supports it" tick the matching items; set "How sure are you?" to Verified where evidence supports it. Captions: "Why? Why? Why?", "Physical, human, system / latent", "Cause area", "Tick the evidence".

## Scene 9: Natural and forced deterioration, views (34 s, 74 words)

**Say:** "One question I always ask about equipment is whether the wear is natural or forced. Silicone on a sealing jaw wears out eventually and that's natural, but running it a year past its life because nothing prompted anyone to change it is forced, which is wear we allowed, so I record that under Type of wear. The same tree also shows as 5 Whys, or a Fishbone grouped by the Cause area I tagged."

[VISUAL] Select the jaw box (A2). Under "Type of wear" tap "Forced deterioration"; show the help line and "Natural deterioration" as the alternative (do not set it). Select the settings box (B1) and tap "Not applicable". Click the tabs "5 Whys" then "Fishbone". The Fishbone shows its "Group by: Level / Cause area" choice and opens by Cause area for a process failure: pause 3 s on it, then click "Level" for 3 s, then back to "Tree". Captions: "Natural, Forced, Not applicable", "Fishbone by Cause area".

## Scene 10: Cause coach and Challenge (48 s, 108 words)

**Say:** "Now the AI help, which I treat as a second pair of eyes, not the boss. The Cause coach suggests causes I may have missed, each with what would confirm or rule it out, and I either Add to tree or Dismiss. It suggests product contaminating the flange, which I hadn't thought of, so that goes in, and I dismiss gas mix because the oxygen readings were in spec. Then Challenge my analysis questions the tree like an experienced reviewer, and it points out that the film root cause has no evidence linked, which is fair, so I link the film specification. The AI suggests, but you decide."

[VISUAL] Scroll to the "Cause coach" card. Click "Suggest causes for" the selected cause (or "Review the whole tree"). Cut while it works ("The coach is thinking."). Show the suggestion cards with the chips and "Would confirm it" / "Would rule it out". Click "Add to tree" on one that is genuinely new; click Dismiss on another. The exact suggestions vary each run; adapt the spoken line (see example-data.md). Then scroll to "Challenge my analysis", run it, cut while it works. Show the chips (for example "No evidence", "Fix first"). Select the film root cause, tick the evidence, mark Verified, then show the point clear. Captions: "Suggestions: add or dismiss", "Challenge my analysis".

## Scene 11: Actions (47 s, 105 words)

**Say:** "Step 5 is Actions, where a lot of RCAs quietly die in a list of "we should". For the jaw that's a Maintenance task to check temperature every week and replace the silicone every 3 months, for the settings it's a Design or equipment change to lock them by recipe, for the film it's a Procedure or standard with supplier change control, and for seal checks it's more frequent checks and a faster leak tester. The total cost is $20,200."

[VISUAL] Actions step. Click "Add corrective action", then "Add preventive action" under each root cause, filling the six actions from example-data.md (one corrective, five preventive) (What will be done?, Corrective or preventive?, Category, Task description, Do this every, Interval unit, Craft or trade, New task or a change?, Owner, Due date, Cost). ## Scene 13: Verify (48 s, 108 words)
