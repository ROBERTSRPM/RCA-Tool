# RCA Pilot full walkthrough: script

Voice only (the owner), calm and practical. Starts from a blank investigation, in the live app (https://www.rcapilot.com). All figures are illustrative; every value typed on screen is in `example-data.md`.

Spelling: Australian/British. Say "RCA Pilot". Say the web address as "w w w dot r c a pilot dot com". Numbers are written out as spoken. Pace about 140 words a minute.

Note for the recording: the AI buttons need a signed-in account. Do not use the AI buttons on real data. AI answers vary each run, so adapt the spoken Cause coach, Challenge and Suggested actions lines to what appears on screen (the lines are written for the likely result).

## Scene 1: The situation (39 s, 87 words)

**Say:** "Ten to three on a Tuesday morning. The overland conveyor at the mine trips on high gearbox oil temperature. The supervisor rings the on-call fitter, who is on site by half past three. By daylight the high-speed bearing in the drive gearbox is in pieces, and the stockpile feed has stopped. Eighteen hours later the belt runs again. And it is the fourth time this year. At the production meeting the general manager asks two questions. Why does this keep happening? And what is it costing us?"

**On screen:** Plain title card, then slow stills or simple graphics: a night shift conveyor, a phone call-out, a gearbox with a failed bearing (stock images or the animation style of the first video). Caption: "Overland conveyor CV-204, drive gearbox. Illustrative example." Last 5 s: text "Why does this keep happening? What is it costing us?"

## Scene 2: What we are trying to achieve (39 s, 86 words)

**Say:** "We want four things. Find the real cause, not just the broken part. Put a dollar figure on the loss, so the fix can compete for budget. Fix it so it stays fixed. And prove it, months later, with evidence. I will do it in RCA Pilot, from a blank page. The numbers are illustrative, made up to show the method. Four failures a year, eighteen hours each, nine hundred tonnes an hour, twelve dollars margin a tonne, and thirty-eight thousand dollars to repair the gearbox."

**On screen:** Four large words appear one by one: "Find the cause", "Size the loss", "Fix it", "Prove it". Then a simple card listing the illustrative inputs: 4 failures a year, 18 hours each, 900 tonnes/hour, $12 margin per tonne, $38,000 repair. Caption: "Illustrative figures".

## Scene 3: Sign in and start (27 s, 58 words)

**Say:** "Go to w w w dot r c a pilot dot com. Demo mode needs no account. I will create an account with an email and password, so my work is kept safely and is there on any device. The AI buttons I use today need you signed in. This is the dashboard, empty. I click New investigation."

**On screen:** Browser at www.rcapilot.com. Click "Create account". Fill Email, Password, Confirm password (use a throwaway recording account, blur the email in the edit). Click "Create account". Show the empty dashboard. Click "New investigation". Caption: "Create account, then New investigation".

## Scene 4: Problem: draft from notes (50 s, 113 words)

**Say:** "Step one is Problem. At the top is a box called Draft problem statement from notes. Today everything is empty. A technician would jot rough notes like these. Now I click Draft problem statement from notes. The AI turns the notes into a clear statement, using only what I wrote. It does not guess a cause, and it lists what the notes left out, under Not in your notes, worth finding out. I can edit the draft, then I press Use this draft. It fills the Describe the problem fields below, and I can undo it. Then I read it and correct anything."

**On screen:** Problem step, all fields empty. The card "Draft problem statement from notes" is open. Click into "Rough notes" and type the shift notes from example-data.md (type at readable speed, or paste). Click "Draft problem statement from notes". Cut while it works ("Writing the draft."). Show the draft box: Title, What happened?, Where on the equipment?, When did it happen?, What was the impact?, and the amber "Not in your notes. Worth finding out:" list. Edit one field by hand. Click "Use this draft". Scroll to the "1. Describe the problem" card now filled; briefly point at the Undo link. Fix the Title and add Asset, Site / area and Date of event (see example-data.md). Captions: "1. Rough notes", "2. AI draft", "3. Use this draft, then edit".

## Scene 5: Problem: asset details (18 s, 37 words)

**Say:** "Below that, Asset details. Tag, type, make and model, duty, criticality, and the failure mode in one phrase. The tag goes into your asset register, and the failure mode links this to similar failures on other assets."

**On screen:** Scroll to "Asset details". Fill Asset tag, pick Asset type "Gearbox", Make and model, Duty or service, tap "A: Critical" under Criticality, pick Failure mode "Bearing failure". Point at the note "It will be added to your asset register". Caption: "Asset details: register and similar failures".

## Scene 6: Loss (57 s, 129 words)

**Say:** "Step two, Loss. If you have no figures yet, choose Not known yet, or enter one rough yearly estimate. A rough number beats none. I enter the five figures from the start of the video, and the calculation runs on the right. Eighteen hours at nine hundred is sixteen thousand, two hundred tonnes. At twelve dollars, that is one hundred and ninety-four thousand, four hundred. Add the repair, and one failure costs two hundred and thirty-two thousand, four hundred. Four a year is nine hundred and twenty-nine thousand, six hundred. The repair is cash. The lost margin is opportunity. Then, what happened to the lost production? Here it was lost for good. Made it up on overtime? Use Made up later. Stock covered it? Use Covered by buffer stock."

**On screen:** Click the Loss step. Show the buttons "I have figures (rough is fine)" and "Not known yet" (hover, do not click, or click and switch back). Fill: How many times a year does it happen? 4; Downtime per event 18; Production rate 900; Unit name tonnes; Margin per unit 12; Repair cost per event 38000; Currency symbol $. Live calculation panel on the right: Lost production per event 16,200 tonnes; Cash costs; Opportunity loss; Cost per event $232,400; Annual loss $929,600; Hours lost 72. Highlight each as it is spoken. Then scroll to "What happened to the production you lost?" Show "Lost for good" at 100%, move the slider briefly to show the other two groups, return to 100%. Captions: "Rough is fine", "Annual loss $929,600 (illustrative)", "Cash vs opportunity".

## Scene 7: Evidence (37 s, 81 words)

**Say:** "Step three, Evidence. Click Add evidence. Each item has a title, a type, a date, and a note on what it shows. A photo of the failed bearing. The oil temperature trend. The inspection of the oil cooler. The oil sample. The maintenance history. And the interviews. Evidence before opinions. Keep the failed parts, and photograph them before anything is cleaned up. Later I link each item to the cause it supports. That is how a theory becomes a proven cause."

**On screen:** Click the Evidence step, then "Add evidence" six times, filling each from example-data.md (Title, Type, Date, Note). For the first item, click "Add a photo" and upload any photo (or skip). Scroll the list so all six titles show with "Not linked to a cause yet" under each. Caption: "Evidence before opinions".

## Scene 8: Cause tree: by hand (52 s, 116 words)

**Say:** "Step four, the Cause tree. The top box is the failure event. I click Why? and type the answer in plain words. The gearbox oil overheated. Why? The oil cooler fins were blocked with dust. That is physical. Why was it blocked? The cooler was not cleaned. That is a human cause. And why was it not cleaned? Because it is on nobody's task list. That is the system level, the latent cause, and I tick This is a root cause. I do two more branches the same way: dust in the oil, and misalignment after the motor change. For each cause I tick the evidence that supports it, and mark it Verified or Ruled out."

**On screen:** Cause tree step, view "Tree". Click the top box and confirm it reads the failure event (type the text from example-data.md). Click "Why?" and type each cause as listed in example-data.md, building branch A fully (Physical, Physical, Human, System / Latent), then branches B and C more quickly (cut or speed up in the edit). On each box use the "Type of cause" buttons: Physical, Human, System / Latent. On the three system boxes, tick "This is a root cause". Under "Evidence that supports it" tick the matching items; set "How sure are you?" to Verified where evidence supports it. Captions: "Why? Why? Why?", "Physical, human, system / latent", "Tick the evidence".

## Scene 9: Natural and forced deterioration, views (29 s, 64 words)

**Say:** "One question I always ask. Is this wear natural, or forced? Bearings wear out eventually. That is natural, managed with monitoring and planned replacement. A bearing that dies early in hot, dirty oil is forced deterioration, wear we caused. Fix the cause and it goes away. I set that under Type of wear. The same tree also shows as 5 Whys, or a Fishbone."

**On screen:** With the cooler-fouling cause selected, scroll to "Type of wear (optional)" and tap "Forced deterioration". Show the help line. Select the top physical box and show "Natural deterioration" as the alternative (do not set it). Click the tabs "5 Whys" then "Fishbone" for about 3 s each, then back to "Tree". Captions: "Natural vs forced", "Tree, 5 Whys, Fishbone".

## Scene 10: Cause coach and Challenge (45 s, 100 words)

**Say:** "Now the AI help. The Cause coach suggests causes I may have missed, each as a hypothesis, with what would confirm it and what would rule it out. I click Suggest causes, read each one, and choose Add to tree or Dismiss. I add one that is new to me. I dismiss belt over-tension, because the motor current shows no overload. Then Challenge my analysis. It questions the tree like an experienced reviewer, from Fix first to Worth a look. It says one root cause has no evidence linked, so I link the maintenance history. The AI suggests. I decide."

**On screen:** Scroll to the "Cause coach" card. Click "Suggest causes for" the selected cause (or "Review the whole tree"). Cut while it works ("The coach is thinking."). Show the suggestion cards with the chips Hypothesis and Natural/Forced deterioration, and "Would confirm it" / "Would rule it out". Click "Add to tree" on one suggestion that is genuinely new; click Dismiss on another. The exact suggestions vary each run. Pick sensible ones and adapt the spoken line (see example-data.md). Then scroll to "Challenge my analysis", click its run button, cut while it works. Show the chips (for example "No evidence", "Fix first"). Use the "no evidence" or "Root cause not verified" point: select that cause, tick the evidence, mark Verified, then show the point disappear or be dismissed. Captions: "Suggestions: add or dismiss", "Challenge my analysis".

## Scene 11: Actions (46 s, 103 words)

**Say:** "Step five, Actions. Corrective actions fix this failure. Preventive actions stop it happening again, one for each root cause, with an owner, due date and cost. For the cooler, the category is Maintenance task: clean the cooler fins every four weeks, by a mechanical fitter. For the oil, Condition monitoring: an oil sample every three months. For alignment, a Procedure or standard. And the spares check shows we hold no spare pinion and bearing set, with a sixteen week lead time, so I add a Spares holding action. These flow into Maintenance plan changes, ready for the planner. Total cost, fifty thousand dollars."

**On screen:** Actions step. Click "Add corrective action", then "Add preventive action" under each root cause, filling the six actions from example-data.md (one corrective, five preventive) (What will be done?, Corrective or preventive?, Category, Task description, Do this every, Interval unit, Craft or trade, New task or a change?, Owner, Due date, Cost). Scroll to "Spares check": Was a spare part needed? Yes; Was it in stock? No; Supplier lead time 112; How was the gap covered? text. Show "Maintenance plan changes" listing tasks with Interval. Show tiles: Total action cost $50,000, Actions 0 of 6 done. Captions: "Corrective and preventive", "Maintenance plan: interval, craft, owner", "Spares".

## Scene 12: CAPA suggestions and quality check (23 s, 48 words)

**Say:** "For each root cause there is also a Suggested actions button. It proposes corrective and preventive actions, says whether the wear looks natural or forced, and nothing is added until I click Add to actions. The Quality check lists any gaps, like a root cause without an action."

**On screen:** On one root-cause group click "Suggested actions" run button, cut while it works, show one suggestion with chips Corrective or Preventive and the line about natural or forced deterioration, click "Add to actions" on one, dismiss another. Scroll to the Quality check panel and show the list; fix one item (for example add a missing interval) and show it clear. Caption: "AI suggests, you accept. Quality check".

## Scene 13: Verify (47 s, 105 words)

**Say:** "Step six, Prove it stopped. Savings only count once every action is Done, the review date is entered, and the failure has not recurred. Months later I mark the actions Done and enter the review date. Recurred? No. Expected events per year after the fix? One, a cautious number. The loss falls to two hundred and thirty-two thousand, four hundred. The saving is six hundred and ninety-seven thousand, two hundred dollars, verified. Payback on fifty thousand is about four weeks, and fifty-four hours of production came back. Then Roll this fix out: the sister gearboxes each get a check, with an owner and a date."

**On screen:** First, go back to Actions and set Status to "Done" on every action. Verify step: Review date 2027-05-18; "Has the failure recurred since the actions were completed?" choose "No, it has not recurred"; Expected events per year after the fix 1; What did you find? (text in example-data.md). Show results: Annual loss before $929,600, after $232,400, "Annual saving (verified)" $697,200, Total action cost $50,000, Payback period about 0.9 months. Scroll to "Roll this fix out": click "Add a similar asset" fields (tag CV-205 then CV-206, duty), "Add to the register", then create the checks and fill "What to check", "Who will check it", "Check due". Captions: "Savings count once verified", "Roll this fix out".

## Scene 14: Report (23 s, 50 words)

**Say:** "Step seven, Report. Draft report writes the full report from everything I have entered. Management summary, one page, is what the general manager reads. I can tell it who the audience is. Then I edit the text and check every fact. When it is right, Print or Save as PDF."

**On screen:** Report step. In the Write-up drafting box type the audience note "The audience is the general manager. Keep it short." Click "Management summary (1 page)", cut while it works, show the draft sections, edit one sentence. Click "Print / Save as PDF" and show the print preview briefly. Captions: "Draft report", "Management summary (1 page)", "Print / Save as PDF".

## Scene 15: Close (24 s, 51 words)

**Say:** "That is the whole method. Describe the problem. Size the loss. Gather evidence. Ask why, down to the system. Fix it with owners and dates. Prove it stopped, and spread the fix. Try it at w w w dot r c a pilot dot com. The link is in the description."

**On screen:** Back to the finished cause tree, then end card: "RCA Pilot by Intelbeyond", "www.rcapilot.com". Optional: seven step names appear in order as the recap is spoken.
