# RCA Pilot full walkthrough: script

Voice only (the owner), calm and practical. Starts from a blank investigation, in the live app (https://www.rcapilot.com). All figures are illustrative; every value typed on screen is in `example-data.md`.

Spelling: Australian/British. Say "RCA Pilot". Say the web address as "w w w dot r c a pilot dot com". All numbers and dollar amounts are written as digits so they are easy to read aloud; read them naturally. Pace about 140 words a minute.

Note for the recording: the AI buttons need a signed-in account. Do not use the AI buttons on real data. AI answers vary each run, so adapt the spoken Cause coach, Challenge and Suggested actions lines to what appears on screen (the lines are written for the likely result).

A retailer complained about slack packs just last month. And this is the 6th time this year. When I ran a plant, this was the call nobody wanted. The general manager asks two questions. Why does this keep happening? And what is it actually costing us?"

Fix it so it stays fixed. And prove it, months later. I'll do it in RCA Pilot, from a blank page. The numbers are illustrative, made up to show the method. 6 events a year. 8 hours of line stoppage each. 3,000 packs an hour. 45 cents margin a pack. $33,100 of cash cost each time."

## Scene 4: Problem: draft from notes (50 s, 113 words)

**Say:** "Step 1 is the Problem. This is where a lot of investigations go wrong. Somebody writes "seals bad" and everyone moves on. First, the Type of failure. A leaking pack is a Process or quality failure. Now, nobody at 4 o'clock in the morning writes a perfect problem statement. A supervisor jots rough notes. So there's a box called Draft problem statement from notes. The AI turns them into a clear statement, using only what I wrote. It doesn't guess a cause. And it tells me what's missing, under Not in your notes, worth finding out. That's your first round of questions. I press Use this draft, then read it and correct anything."

[VISUAL] Problem step, all fields empty. Under "Type of failure" click "Process or quality failure". The card "Draft problem statement from notes" is open. Click into "Rough notes" and type the shift notes from example-data.md (readable speed, or paste). Click "Draft problem statement from notes". Cut while it works ("Writing the draft."). Show the draft box: Title, What happened?, Where in the process?, When did it happen?, What was the impact?, and the amber "Not in your notes. Worth finding out:" list. Edit one field by hand. Click "Use this draft". Scroll to the "1. Describe the problem" card now filled; point at the Undo link. Fix the Title and add Line or process, Site / area and Date of event (see example-data.md). Captions: "1. Rough notes", "2. AI draft", "3. Use this draft, then edit".

## Scene 7: Evidence (38 s, 83 words)

**Say:** "Step 3 is Evidence. And I'll say it the way I would in a plant meeting. Evidence before opinions. Everyone has a theory about those seals. So each piece of evidence gets a title, a type, a date, and a note on what it shows. A photo of a leaking pack. The sealing temperature log. The jaw inspection. The settings and changeover record. The film specification. The interviews. One tip. Keep the retained packs and worn parts, and photograph them before anything's cleaned."

[VISUAL] Click the Evidence step, then "Add evidence" six times, filling each from example-data.md (Title, Type, Date, Note). For the first item click "Add a photo" and upload any photo (or skip). Scroll the list so all six titles show with "Not linked to a cause yet" under each. Caption: "Evidence before opinions".

## Scene 8: Cause tree: by hand (68 s, 153 words)

**Say:** "Now Step 4, the Cause tree. This is where the thinking happens. The top box is the failure event. Then we keep asking Why? in plain words. Why were the packs leaking? The seals were weak along the flange. Why? The jaw ran 17 degrees low, with worn silicone. That's physical. But stop there and you'll be back soon. Why wasn't it caught? Nothing in the maintenance plan checks jaw temperature, or replaces that silicone. That's the system level. That's the root. I mark it with This is a root cause. Then the other branches. Wrong settings, because operators raised speed and cut dwell to keep up. A new film batch. And a seal check of only 3 packs every 2 hours, so a bad seal can run for hours. For each cause, I tick the evidence and mark it Verified or Ruled out. If I can't prove it, it stays a theory."

[VISUAL] Cause tree step, view "Tree". Click the top box and type the failure event from example-data.md. Click "Why?" and type each cause as listed, building branch A fully, then B, C and D more quickly (cut or speed up in the edit). Use the "Type of cause" buttons: Physical, Human, System / Latent. Also tap one "Cause area" button (Equipment, Materials, Method, Measurement, People, Environment) on each cause, as listed in example-data.md; it shows on the card and groups the Fishbone. On the four system boxes tick "This is a root cause". Under "Evidence that supports it" tick the matching items; set "How sure are you?" to Verified where evidence supports it. Captions: "Why? Why? Why?", "Physical, human, system / latent", "Cause area", "Tick the evidence".

## Scene 9: Natural and forced deterioration, views (37 s, 82 words)

**Say:** "Here's a question I always ask when equipment's involved. Is this wear natural, or forced? Silicone on a sealing jaw wears out eventually. That's natural. But running it a year past its life, because nothing prompted anyone to change it? That's forced. Wear we allowed. I record that under Type of wear. For the recipe settings, I pick Not applicable. The same tree also shows as 5 Whys, or a Fishbone grouped by the Cause area I tagged. Same facts, different angle."

[VISUAL] Select the jaw box (A2). Under "Type of wear" tap "Forced deterioration"; show the help line and "Natural deterioration" as the alternative (do not set it). Select the settings box (B1) and tap "Not applicable". Click the tabs "5 Whys" then "Fishbone". The Fishbone shows its "Group by: Level / Cause area" choice and opens by Cause area for a process failure: pause 3 s on it, then click "Level" for 3 s, then back to "Tree". Captions: "Natural, Forced, Not applicable", "Fishbone by Cause area".

## Scene 10: Cause coach and Challenge (45 s, 101 words)

**Say:** "Now, the AI help. It's a second pair of eyes, not the boss. The Cause coach suggests causes I may have missed, each a hypothesis, with what would confirm it and rule it out. I either Add to tree, or Dismiss. Product contaminating the flange? Hadn't thought of that. In it goes. Gas mix I dismiss, because the oxygen readings were in spec. Then Challenge my analysis. It questions the tree like an experienced reviewer. It says the film root cause has no evidence linked. Fair. So I link the film specification and mark it Verified. The AI suggests. You decide."

[VISUAL] Scroll to the "Cause coach" card. Click "Suggest causes for" the selected cause (or "Review the whole tree"). Cut while it works ("The coach is thinking."). Show the suggestion cards with the chips and "Would confirm it" / "Would rule it out". Click "Add to tree" on one that is genuinely new; click Dismiss on another. The exact suggestions vary each run; adapt the spoken line (see example-data.md). Then scroll to "Challenge my analysis", run it, cut while it works. Show the chips (for example "No evidence", "Fix first"). Select the film root cause, tick the evidence, mark Verified, then show the point clear. Captions: "Suggestions: add or dismiss", "Challenge my analysis".

## Scene 11: Actions (47 s, 104 words)

**Say:** "Step 5, Actions. This is where a lot of RCAs quietly die. Great analysis, then a list of "we should". So every action gets an owner, a due date and a cost. Corrective actions fix this failure. Preventive actions stop it coming back. For the jaw, a Maintenance task. Check temperature with a probe every week, and replace the silicone every 3 months. For the settings, a Design or equipment change. Lock them by recipe. For the film, a Procedure or standard. Supplier change control. For seal checks, more frequent checks with records, and a faster leak tester. Total cost? $20,200. Hold that number."

[VISUAL] Actions step. Click "Add corrective action", then "Add preventive action" under each root cause, filling the six actions from example-data.md (one corrective, five preventive) (What will be done?, Corrective or preventive?, Category, Task description, Do this every, Interval unit, Craft or trade, New task or a change?, Owner, Due date, Cost).
