# RCA Pilot full walkthrough: script

Voice only (the owner), calm and practical. Starts from a blank investigation, in the live app (https://www.rcapilot.com). All figures are illustrative; every value typed on screen is in `example-data.md`.

Spelling: Australian/British. Say "RCA Pilot". Say the web address as "w w w dot r c a pilot dot com". All numbers and dollar amounts are written as digits so they are easy to read aloud; read them naturally. Pace about 140 words a minute.

Note for the recording: the AI buttons need a signed-in account. Do not use the AI buttons on real data. AI answers vary each run, so adapt the spoken Cause coach, Challenge and Suggested actions lines to what appears on screen (the lines are written for the likely result).

## Scene 1: The situation (48 s, 107 words)

**Say:** "4:00 on a Thursday morning, night shift. At the end of Line 3, it is the 2-hourly seal check. The operator takes 3 packs of chicken and rice and drops them in the leak tester. 2 of them leak along the seal. Everything packed since the last good check goes on hold. About 6,000 packs. The phone rings at the production manager's house. Then the QA manager's. A retailer has already complained about slack packs last month. And it is the 6th time this year. At the meeting, the general manager asks two questions. Why does this keep happening? And what is it costing us?"

[SFX] Open on the steady hum and rhythmic thump of a tray sealer on a food line, with the clink of trays and a fan. Under the second line, a faint bubbling from the leak tester.

[MUSIC] Quiet, tense, low pad. Starts at 0:03, no drums.

[VISUAL] Plain title card, then slow stills or simple graphics: a night shift tray sealer under bright light, end-of-line check table, an operator at the end of the line pressing a pack under water in a leak tester with bubbles rising from the flange, a slack pack with a loose lid, red product-hold tags going on pallets, a bedside phone lighting up at night, then a meeting room. Caption: "Line 3 tray sealer, chilled ready meals. Illustrative example." Last 5 s: text "Why does this keep happening? What is it costing us?"

[SFX] Phone ringing at night on "The phone rings", a second ring tone on "Then the QA manager's". Cut all sound on the final question, then silence for one beat.

[MUSIC] Fades out on the last question.

## Scene 2: What we are trying to achieve (39 s, 87 words)

**Say:** "We want four things. Find the real cause, not just the faulty pack. Put a dollar figure on the loss, so the fix can compete for budget. Fix it so it stays fixed. And prove it, months later, with evidence. I will do it in RCA Pilot, from a blank page. The numbers are illustrative, made up to show the method. 6 events a year, 8 hours of line stoppage each, 3,000 packs an hour, 45 cents margin a pack, and $33,100 of cash cost each time."

[VISUAL] Four large words appear one by one: "Find the cause", "Size the loss", "Fix it", "Prove it". Then a simple card listing the illustrative inputs: 6 events a year, 8 hours each, 3,000 packs/hour, $0.45 margin per pack, $33,100 cash cost per event. Caption: "Illustrative figures".

[MUSIC] Tension resolves into a calm, light bed at the first word.

Then the box called Draft problem statement from notes. A supervisor would jot rough notes like these. I click Draft problem statement from notes. The AI turns them into a clear statement, using only what I wrote. It does not guess a cause, and it lists what the notes left out, under Not in your notes, worth finding out. I edit the draft, press Use this draft, and then read it and correct anything."

[VISUAL] Problem step, all fields empty. Under "Type of failure" click "Process or quality failure". The card "Draft problem statement from notes" is open. ## Scene 6: Loss (54 s, 122 words)

**Say:** "Step 2, Loss. If you have no figures yet, choose Not known yet, or enter one rough yearly estimate. A rough number beats none. I enter 6 events a year, 8 hours each, 3,000 packs an hour, and 45 cents margin a pack. 8 hours at 3,000 is 24,000 packs not made. At 45 cents, that is $10,800 of lost margin, which is opportunity. Then the cash costs. The call-out is $1,200. Under Other costs I use the quick-add presets: Scrapped product $14,500. Product on hold $3,200. Customer claims and credits $6,800. Retailer penalties $5,000. Rework $2,400. Cash is $33,100. One event costs $43,900. 6 a year is $263,400. The line runs at capacity, so the lost output is lost for good."

Keep the retained packs and the worn parts, and photograph them before anything is cleaned or replaced. Later I link each item to the cause it supports."

[VISUAL] Click the Evidence step, then "Add evidence" six times, filling each from example-data.md (Title, Type, Date, Note). For the first item click "Add a photo" and upload any photo (or skip). Scroll the list so all six titles show with "Not linked to a cause yet" under each. Caption: "Evidence before opinions".

## Scene 8: Cause tree: by hand (60 s, 135 words)

**Say:** "Step 4, the Cause tree. The top box is the failure event. I click Why? and type the answer in plain words. Why were the packs leaking? The seals were weak along the flange. Why? The jaw ran 17 degrees low, with worn silicone. That is physical. Why was it not caught? Nothing in the maintenance plan checks jaw temperature or replaces the silicone. That is the system level, and I tick This is a root cause. Three more branches the same way. Wrong settings, where operators raised speed and cut dwell to keep up. A new film batch. And a seal check of only 3 packs every 2 hours, so a bad seal runs for hours before anyone knows. For each cause I tick the supporting evidence and mark it Verified or Ruled out."

[VISUAL] Cause tree step, view "Tree". Click the top box and type the failure event from example-data.md. Click "Why?" and type each cause as listed, building branch A fully, then B, C and D more quickly (cut or speed up in the edit). Use the "Type of cause" buttons: Physical, Human, System / Latent. On the four system boxes tick "This is a root cause". Under "Evidence that supports it" tick the matching items; set "How sure are you?" to Verified where evidence supports it. Captions: "Why? Why? Why?", "Physical, human, system / latent", "Tick the evidence".

## Scene 9: Natural and forced deterioration, views (34 s, 74 words)

**Say:** "One question I always ask where equipment is involved. Is this wear natural, or forced? Silicone on a sealing jaw wears out eventually. That is natural. But running it a year past its life, because nothing prompted anyone, is forced: wear we allowed. I set that under Type of wear. For the recipe settings, wear does not apply, so I choose Not applicable. The same tree also shows as 5 Whys, or a Fishbone."

[VISUAL] Select the jaw box (A2). Under the deterioration choice tap "Forced"; show the help line and "Natural" as the alternative (do not set it). Select the settings box (B1) and tap "Not applicable". Click the tabs "5 Whys" then "Fishbone" for about 3 s each, then back to "Tree". Captions: "Natural, Forced, Not applicable", "Tree, 5 Whys, Fishbone".

## Scene 10: Cause coach and Challenge (46 s, 102 words)

**Say:** "Now the AI help. The Cause coach suggests causes I may have missed, each as a hypothesis, with what would confirm it and what would rule it out. I click Suggest causes, read each one, and choose Add to tree or Dismiss. I add one that is new to me, product contaminating the flange. I dismiss gas mix, because the oxygen readings were in spec. Then Challenge my analysis. It questions the tree like an experienced reviewer. It says the film root cause has no evidence linked, so I link the film specification and mark it Verified. The AI suggests. I decide."

[VISUAL] Scroll to the "Cause coach" card. Click "Suggest causes for" the selected cause (or "Review the whole tree"). Cut while it works ("The coach is thinking."). Show the suggestion cards with the chips and "Would confirm it" / "Would rule it out". Click "Add to tree" on one that is genuinely new; click Dismiss on another. The exact suggestions vary each run; adapt the spoken line (see example-data.md). Then scroll to "Challenge my analysis", run it, cut while it works. Show the chips (for example "No evidence", "Fix first"). Select the film root cause, tick the evidence, mark Verified, then show the point clear. Captions: "Suggestions: add or dismiss", "Challenge my analysis".
