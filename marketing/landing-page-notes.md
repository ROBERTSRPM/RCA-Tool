# Landing page review (https://www.rcapilot.com, from web/app/page.tsx)

Review only. The app has not been touched. Suggestions are ranked by likely effect on conversion. Pricing mentioned here is a DRAFT: A$2,500 90-day site pilot, then A$450 a month per site, ex GST.

## What works
- The page is clean, loads one idea at a time, and the screenshots show the real product.
- The demo video sits high on the page.
- "Try the sample investigation" needs no sign-up. That keeps the barrier low.
- "Prove the countermeasure held" is the right promise. It is the difference from a template.
- The credibility line says who built it.

## Top changes (do these first)

1. **There is no way to say "I'm interested" for a site.** The only contacts are the sample, the account button and a footer email. For a B2B tool that sells as a pilot, add a clear "Talk to the founder by email" button: `mailto:roberts@intelbeyond.com?subject=Pilot%20at%20our%20site`. Put it in the hero and the closing band. Email only. Do not add a phone number or booking link.
2. **The page does not say who it is for.** "Get your plant out of firefighting mode" is memorable but vague. A visitor must read down to learn it's for maintenance and plant teams. Add a one-line audience under the headline: "For maintenance, reliability and operations teams at food, beverage and process plants."
3. **No "pilot for sites" section.** Add a short band: "Start with one site. A 90-day pilot, with set-up and help on your first RCAs." Show the draft price only once it is final. Until then, say "Pricing on request by email" so the page does not make visitors guess.
4. **The credibility line is the strongest asset and it's buried.** Move it up, directly under the hero, and make it specific: "Built by Roberts, a former Maintenance, Production and Site Manager." A name and a role beat "a former manager". Consider a short sentence about a real failure he ran.
5. **Lead with the dollar figure.** The demo thumbnail option B ("$238K a year. One pump.") is the stronger hook for managers. Try it in the hero sub-line: "Put a dollar figure on repeat failures, and prove the countermeasure held." Use an A/B test on the headline once there is traffic.

## Copy changes

- Sub-line: "takes your team from failure to verified solution, with an AI reviewer that suggests causes and actions while your people decide." Long. Try: "Cost the failure. Find the cause. Prove the countermeasure held. The AI suggests. Your people decide."
- The headline-option comment in the code uses a "fix it" headline. Use "Find the real cause. Put in a countermeasure that holds." to follow the countermeasure wording.
- "Watch the 3-minute demo": actual length is 2:55, which is fine. Keep it.
- "No account needed to try it." Good, keep.
- Step 03 body is good. Add "Close the loop" wording to the heading if you want a stronger action verb.
- AI section: say what it does not do. One line: "Nothing is added until you accept it. Nothing is marked verified because of a suggestion." That is already in the video description. Putting it on the page answers the first question from any QA or IT person.

## Missing proof and trust items

- No screenshot or quote from a real user yet. Leave a slot for the first design partner quote. Until then, do not invent one.
- No data location. Add "Data held in Australia (Sydney)" near the footer only once confirmed on the privacy page.
- No security or "how we use AI" link in the footer. Add one once the page exists.
- The footer has Help, Privacy and Terms. Privacy and terms are marked as drafts internally. Have them reviewed before charging.
- No name for who is behind it beyond "Intelbeyond". Add a short About: photo optional (voice only has been the owner's preference), a name, and a two-line background.

## Segment fit

- The page has no food and beverage language. The 4am leaking packs story is the strongest hook for the first segment. Consider a section called "Repeat failures on a packing line", with the story in 3 lines and a sample investigation that matches it. The current sample is a slurry pump, which suits mining and process more than FMCG. Worth adding a packaging-line sample.
- Mention NCR and CAPA references on the page. The product supports a quality reference field. QA leads search for that.

## Page structure

Current order: hero, demo, credibility, how it works, AI reviewer, closing band. Suggested order:
1. Hero with audience line and two buttons: try the sample, and email the founder.
2. Credibility line.
3. Demo video.
4. How it works (3 steps).
5. AI reviewer.
6. Pilot offer for sites.
7. Short FAQ: where is data held, does the AI train on our data, how do we get started, can we export.
8. Closing band with both buttons.

## Small things
- The hero image is cropped to 330px on a phone. Check it on a real phone. Most LinkedIn clicks will come from mobile.
- The two hero buttons plus a text link might be one too many. Keep the sample as the main one and the email button as the second. Move the demo link lower.
- Add a short meta description and an Open Graph image so links shared on LinkedIn show a good card (use the demo thumbnail or the loss card). Check what a LinkedIn share currently shows.
- Add simple privacy-friendly analytics to measure clicks on the sample, the email button and the demo play. Add a cookie notice only if the analytics need one.
- Page weight: images are marked unoptimized. Check load time on a mobile connection.

## Measure it
Track in a week: visits, sample opens, demo plays, email clicks, sign-ups. With low traffic, small numbers still tell you which button gets used.
