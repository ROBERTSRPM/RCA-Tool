# Ideal customer profile (draft for owner review)

Pricing referenced here is a DRAFT: A$2,500 90-day site pilot, then A$450 a month per site, ex GST. Not final.

## Who we sell to

A site, not a person. The buyer is a site that has the same failures coming back and no good way to show what they cost or whether the countermeasure held.

## Segment 1: Australian food and beverage / FMCG manufacturing (start here)

Why first:
- The owner has run these operations. The 4am leaking packs story is this segment's story.
- Retailer complaints, audits and recalls put a dollar and a deadline on repeat failures.
- Packing lines have many small repeat stops, so there is always a live RCA to run.
- Sites already log NCRs and CAPAs, so "verified countermeasure" lands.

Sub-segments to list first: chilled and ready meals, bakery, dairy, beverages, snack and confectionery, meat and poultry processing, packaging and co-packers.

## Segment 2: other process plants (after 3 or more food wins)

Chemicals and plastics, building materials, water and wastewater, pharma and nutraceutical, mid-size mineral processing, pulp and paper. Same pain, but the language and assets differ. Do not chase these until Segment 1 gives a case study.

## Who to target (job titles)

| Role | Why they care | Role in the deal |
|---|---|---|
| Maintenance manager | Owns repeat breakdowns and gets blamed for them | Champion, often the buyer |
| Reliability engineer | Runs RCAs, hates chasing actions in spreadsheets | Champion and daily user |
| Operations or plant manager | Owns the budget and the loss figure | Buyer and signer |
| CI (continuous improvement) lead | Needs structure and a record of results | Champion |
| QA / food safety lead | Owns NCRs, CAPAs, audits and retailer complaints | Influencer, sometimes buyer |

Start with maintenance managers and reliability engineers. They feel it most and can move fastest. Bring in the plant manager for the pilot sign-off.

## Company size

- Best fit: a single site with about 50 to 500 employees, running 2 or 3 shifts, with a maintenance team of 5 to 40 people.
- Likely annual revenue A$20M to A$500M.
- Both an independent site and a site inside a bigger group are fine. In a group, sell to the site first. Avoid starting with head office procurement.
- Too small: under about 30 staff and no dedicated maintenance. Too big for now: global groups that need enterprise security reviews, SSO and procurement panels.

## Triggers that make them buy

1. Repeat failures. The same line, pump or sealer is down again, for the third or sixth time this year.
2. Audit coming up (SQF, BRCGS, customer audit). They need evidence that corrective actions are closed and checked.
3. Retailer complaint or penalty. A customer has raised a complaint, a claim or a credit.
4. A recall, hold or near miss that got management attention.
5. A new maintenance or plant manager who wants to show control in the first 90 days.
6. A budget round. Someone has to explain what downtime cost last year.
7. A reliability or CI initiative that has stalled because actions are not followed up.

Good question to open with: "What failure did you have to explain to management last month?"

## Objections and answers

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "We already do RCAs in Word and Excel." | Fine. The trouble is not the template. It is the list of "we should" that nobody checks. RCA Pilot puts a dollar figure on the failure and keeps the countermeasure open until it is verified. |
| "We have a CMMS / SAP." | RCA Pilot sits beside it. The CMMS records work. This records why it failed, what it cost and whether the countermeasure held. You can keep your work order numbers in it. |
| "We have no time." | An RCA can start from rough shift notes. A rough dollar figure is enough. In the pilot, the owner sets up your asset register and helps with the first one or two RCAs. |
| "AI on plant data. Is it safe?" | The AI only suggests. Your people accept or dismiss every item. Nothing is marked verified because of a suggestion. Investigation text goes to the AI provider only to answer that request. Check the provider's current terms and the site's privacy page before quoting more. |
| "Where is our data held?" | In Australia (Sydney region). Confirm this is still true before saying it (LAUNCH-PLAN notes it as a goal for the privacy page). |
| "It's one person's side project. What if it disappears?" | Be straight about it. Data can be exported at any time. The owner has run plants and uses the tool on real failures. A 90-day pilot limits the risk on both sides. |
| "A$2,500 and A$450 a month (draft) is more than we can sign off." | One avoided breakdown on a critical asset usually costs more than a year's fee. The pilot fee is set to sit under a manager's own approval limit. Check the site's limit before the conversation. |
| "Our IT will want a security review." | A short security summary is on the owner's list (see owner-actions.md). Until then, say so and offer it by email. |
| "We tried RCA software before and nobody used it." | Ask what stopped them. Usually it was too heavy or nobody owned follow-up. The pilot is built around one or two real failures, not a rollout to everyone. |
| "Will it replace our reliability engineer?" | No. It makes their analysis quicker and keeps a record. The AI suggests, they decide. |

## Where to find them

- The owner's own contacts first: ex-colleagues, suppliers, contractors, people from past sites.
- LinkedIn search: titles above, filtered to Australia, with industry "Food and Beverage Manufacturing" or "Food Production".
- Industry bodies and events (see first-50-plan.md).
- Referrals from each design partner.

## Disqualify early

- No site-level authority and no one who owns the problem.
- Wants a full CMMS or EAM replacement.
- Wants a global rollout with a procurement process before trying one site.
