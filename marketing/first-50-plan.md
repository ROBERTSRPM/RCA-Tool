# First 50 plan: 6 weeks (draft for owner review)

Sized for 5 to 8 hours a week around a full-time job. Nothing is posted or sent without the owner's review.
Pricing in this plan is a DRAFT: A$2,500 90-day site pilot, then A$450 a month per site, ex GST.

Goal for the 6 weeks: 5 design partners signed up, and the first conversations with a second wave of cold contacts. Fifty paying users is a longer game. Treat this as the first leg.

Dates assume Monday 23 November 2026 as week 1. Weeks 5 and 6 fall over Christmas and New Year. Many plants shut down or run short-staffed then. Expect fewer replies, and use those weeks for follow-ups, content and tidy-up.

## The design-partner offer (DRAFT, owner decides)

For 5 sites:
- The 90-day pilot, with the owner setting up the asset register and co-leading the first 1 or 2 real RCAs.
- Pilot fee waived or reduced in return for: using it on real failures, written feedback by email every two weeks, and a short case study (with the site's approval and naming only if they agree).
- Owner decision: the old LAUNCH-PLAN says "free for 3 months". The pricing draft says A$2,500 pilot. Pick one: fee waived for design partners, half price, or full price with credit. Avoid the word "free" in all copy.
- Owner decision: the old plan says a 30-minute feedback call every two weeks. Email only is the rule. Offer written feedback by default, and a short meeting only if the partner asks for one.
- Cap it at 5. Say so. A cap is honest and makes a decision easier.

## Tracking (set up in week 1, 30 minutes)

One Google Sheet, one row per person. Tabs: People, Sites, Content.

People columns: Name, Role, Company, Site, Segment, Source (ex-colleague, warm, cold, referral), LinkedIn URL, Email, Stage, Last contact, Next step, Next date, Notes.

Stages: Not contacted, Note sent, Connected, DM sent, Replied, Demo sent, Trial started, Pilot offered, Pilot signed, Not now, No.

Every Friday, 15 minutes: update stages, count the week's numbers, set next week's follow-ups. A CRM is not needed until about 50 active rows.

## Weekly plan

### Week 1 (23 Nov): set up and warm list
- Build the sheet. Pull 40 names from your network: ex-colleagues, ex-suppliers, ex-contractors, people at food and beverage sites. Mark each Warm or Ex-colleague. (1.5 h)
- Update your LinkedIn profile: headline and About, naming the problem you solve. Add the demo video as a featured link. (1 h)
- Send 10 personal messages to the closest ex-colleagues. Use the templates. Ask for advice and a view on the demo, not a sale. (1.5 h)
- Publish post 1 (the 4am story) after review. (1 h)
- Check the product: sign in, run the sample, make sure the demo path works for someone else. Check the support email is monitored. (1 h)
- Metric: 40 names in the sheet, 10 messages sent, 3 replies.

### Week 2 (30 Nov): warm network
- Send 15 more notes and DMs to warm contacts and ex-colleagues. (2 h)
- Follow up the week 1 non-replies once, with a different angle. (0.5 h)
- Post 2 and post 3. (1.5 h)
- Reply to every comment on posts within a day. (0.5 h)
- Send the design-partner invitation to the 5 to 8 best-fit warm contacts. (1 h)
- Metric: 25 contacts reached in total, 8 replies, 3 people who have watched the demo or tried the sample.

### Week 3 (7 Dec): groups, content, first sessions
- Join 3 to 5 Australian groups and associations from the list below (verify each first). Read before posting. (1 h)
- Post 4 and post 5. Share post 5 or the demo in one relevant group where self-promotion is allowed. (1.5 h)
- Send 15 cold connection notes to maintenance managers and reliability engineers at food and beverage sites. (1.5 h)
- Do the first guided demos, by screen share only if the contact asks. Otherwise send the 3-minute demo and the sample link. (1.5 h)
- Metric: 40 contacts reached in total, 2 design partner conversations in progress, 1 person has run the sample.

### Week 4 (14 Dec): convert
- Turn the best conversations into pilot offers. Send the pilot summary by email. (2 h)
- Follow up cold notes from week 3. (1 h)
- Post 6 and post 7. (1.5 h)
- Answer "how much?" with the draft pricing reply. (0.5 h)
- Collect one question per person: what stopped them? Put it in the sheet. (0.5 h)
- Metric: 2 design partners agreed in principle. 20 cold contacts reached.

### Week 5 (21 Dec): holiday week, light
- Sign the first design partners. Set up accounts and asset registers. (2 h)
- Post 8 only. (0.5 h)
- Follow up replies and send a short seasonal note to warm contacts: "Back in the new year, happy to set up your first RCA in January." (1 h)
- Plan the January round: who is on shutdown, who is back when. (0.5 h)
- Metric: 3 design partners signed or committed, all with a date for their first RCA.

### Week 6 (28 Dec): review and set January
- Light week. Post 9 and post 10 can be scheduled to go out in early January. (1 h)
- Review the sheet: replies per channel, which message worked, which segment answered. (1 h)
- Write down what each design partner needs from the product and tell the PM. (1 h)
- Draft the first case study outline from the first partner's progress. (1 h)
- Metric: 5 design partners signed or in final discussion, the January list ready with 40 new names.

## Channels

### Your own network (60% of the effort)
Ex-colleagues first. They know you, they know the 4am call, and they will tell you if the tool is wrong. Ask each for two things: an honest view, and an introduction to one person at another site.

### LinkedIn content (about 1.5 hours a week)
Two posts a week in weeks 1 to 4, then one a week. Posts in `linkedin-posts.md`. Real plant stories, one idea per post. Link the demo only where it fits. Reply to comments the same day if you can.

### Australian groups and associations
Verify each of these before joining or quoting. Websites and memberships change.
- Engineers Australia, including its Mechanical college. (Exists. Check which technical groups and local events are active.)
- Asset Management Council (Australian asset management society, linked to Engineers Australia). Has local chapters and events. Verify current chapter list and event dates.
- Australian Institute of Food Science and Technology (AIFST). Events and networking. Verify.
- Australian Food and Grocery Council (AFGC). Mostly an industry body for manufacturers, useful for context and events more than outreach. Verify.
- Food Innovation Australia Limited (FIAL). Food and beverage industry growth body. Verify.
- Australian Institute of Packaging (AIP). Packing line people. Verify.
- Trade shows: Foodpro (Australian food processing and packaging show). Verify the date and whether it runs in 2027.
- LinkedIn groups: search for maintenance, reliability and food manufacturing groups in Australia. Names change, so check the member count and whether posts are active.
- Not sure these exist in current form, so verify before naming: any state maintenance engineering societies, the International Conference of Maintenance Societies (ICOMS).

Rule for groups: be useful first. Share a story or a lesson. Mention the tool only where the group allows it.

## Success measures at the end of 6 weeks

- 5 design partner sites signed or in final discussion.
- 100 or more people contacted.
- 15 or more replies.
- 20 or more people who have watched the demo or run the sample.
- At least 5 written objections collected, with the answer that worked.

## If something is not working

- No replies from warm contacts: the message is too long or sounds like a pitch. Cut it to three lines and ask only for advice.
- Replies but no trial: the sample investigation is too far from their problem. Offer to load one of their real failures.
- Trial but no pilot: ask what blocked them. It is usually time, price or IT. Fix the right one.
